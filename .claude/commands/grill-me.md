description: Pressure-test a decision one question at a time until every branch is resolved
---

Run a relentless, structured interview on a decision she's weighing — a hire, a budget, a process or systems change, a new report, a vendor, a policy. Walk the full decision tree, one question at a time, until every branch is resolved.

This is NOT a critique. It's a collaborative interrogation — the second-in-command who won't let a half-baked decision go to the board.

Usage: `/grill-me [decision]`

Examples:
- `/grill-me whether to bring payroll in-house`
- `/grill-me the new budgeting process`
- `/grill-me switching accounting systems`
- `/grill-me adding a financial analyst to the team`
- `/grill-me this number before it goes in the board pack`

---

## Step 1: Load context

Before asking anything, read what already exists:
- `Context/about.md`, `Context/team.md` — her role, company, the team
- Anything in `Reports/`, `Deadlines/`, or `Committees/` related to the decision

If a question can be answered from those files, answer it yourself — don't waste her time.

## Step 2: Establish scope

State back what you're grilling in one sentence:
> "Grilling: [clear statement of the decision]"

Then ask for the 30-second version: what's the decision, what's driving it, what does a good outcome look like?

## Step 3: Run the interview

**One question per message. Never stack questions.** Each answer informs the next.

For every question, give your recommended answer first: "My take: [X], because [Y]." Then let her confirm or override. Never ask a naked question — she wants a thinking partner, not a survey.

Work top-down. Don't ask about cost before the problem is clear. Branches to cover (adapt to the decision):

**Problem / foundation**
- What problem does this actually solve? Whose problem is it?
- What does success look like in numbers (cost saved, time saved, risk reduced)?
- What happens if she does nothing?

**Options**
- What are the real alternatives, including the cheap/simple one?
- What's the minimum viable version?

**Cost & risk**
- What does it cost — money, team time, her attention?
- What's the downside if it goes wrong? Who's exposed (board, auditors, SARS, cashflow)?
- What controls / compliance angle does this touch?

**Execution**
- Who owns it? What's the first concrete step?
- What's the dependency or the thing that could stall it?
- What's the timeline, and does it collide with close / year-end / a statutory deadline?

Flag contradictions immediately. Push on vague answers ("we'll sort that later" → "that's load-bearing — resolve it or log it as a risk"). If her thinking is tight, don't manufacture objections — say "this is solid, [N] more questions" and wrap.

## Step 4: Deliver the summary

```
## Grill Complete: [decision] — [date]

### The decision (post-grill)
[2-3 sentences as it now stands]

### Decisions made
1. [decision] — reason

### Key risks
1. [risk] — mitigation

### Assumptions to test
1. [assumption] — how to test

### Open items
[anything unresolved]

### Next steps
1. [first concrete action]
```

Offer to save it (e.g. into `Committees/` if it's heading to a meeting, or a decision note). Don't write into files unless she says yes.

## Rules

- Be relentless, not rude. Push hard on weak spots, stay collaborative.
- One question at a time — non-negotiable. Stacking lets the hard ones get dodged.
- Always recommend. She wants you to think, not just ask.
- Tie it to her world — cost, controls, deadlines, the board — not generic MBA questions.
- Respect momentum. If it's tight, wrap it up fast.
